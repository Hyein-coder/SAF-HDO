## Model families

“Catalyst-agnostic” needs a careful definition here: a model may be *formally parameterized* without catalyst identity, yet still be calibrated to one catalyst and fail when transferred. The literature I found offers little validated transfer across sulfided and noble-metal catalysts for raw bio-oil. The clearest cross-catalyst evidence is for *comparing* catalysts in model-compound microkinetics, and for ML that explicitly includes catalyst features; neither by itself establishes universal transferability.

## What has evidence of transferability?

- **Explicit multi-catalyst comparison:** the DFT microkinetic study evaluates Co, Ni, Cu, Pd, and Pt, but predicts distinct catalytic behavior rather than a single catalyst-independent rate law. It is evidence that descriptor-based atomistic models *can encode catalyst changes*, not that a model trained on one metal transfers to sulfided NiMo/CoMo.
- **Multi-catalyst-aware ML:** the 2024 guaiacol study includes catalyst surface area and crystallinity along with operating conditions. It supports learning catalyst-feature/performance associations within a literature dataset; its scope is model-compound guaiacol, not raw-oil SAF-range distribution.
- **Bulk severity/H₂ correlations:** useful for screening and mass-balance constraints, but observed on particular feed/catalyst systems. Treating them as universal would overstate the evidence.
- **No verified universal model found:** I found no published raw-pyrolysis-oil HDO model demonstrated across sulfided catalysts, noble metals, and Ni while predicting C5–C20+ selectivity.

## Recommendation for the three-stage flowsheet

1. **Best near-term path: structured lumped kinetic model with stage-specific parameters.** Track oxygenate/reactivity classes plus hydrocarbon carbon-number or boiling-range bins, with rates driven by temperature, H₂ partial pressure, and residence time/LHSV. Fit to stage-relevant data and retain catalyst identity as a parameter. It is the most practical step beyond fixed yields while keeping a tractable flowsheet model. The raw-oil lumped literature provides a starting structure, but the C-number bins and pressure dependence would need to be developed and calibrated for your data.
2. **Best longer-term chemistry: molecular reconstruction plus reaction-network/Monte Carlo model.** It can connect feed structure and reaction rules to product composition and H₂ use. It is more data- and computation-intensive and needs catalyst-specific kinetics or validated descriptor relationships before claiming cross-catalyst transfer.
3. **Best pragmatic hybrid: ML surrogate constrained by material balances and kinetic/severity structure.** Train on multi-catalyst, multi-condition data with catalyst descriptors, feed descriptors, and stage conditions; predict C-number bins, oxygen removal, and H₂ consumption. Use it inside or alongside Aspen, with explicit out-of-domain checks. Existing ML evidence is currently model-compound-focused, so a raw-oil dataset is the key prerequisite.
